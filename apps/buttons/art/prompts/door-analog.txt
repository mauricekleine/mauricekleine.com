An illustration plate from a 1947 American mail-order catalog (in the manner of the Sears, Roebuck and Montgomery Ward catalogs), painted in gouache and watercolor over confident ink linework by a skilled commercial illustrator.

Scene: the front hallway of a modest American home on a bright morning. A man stands at the front door, about to leave for the day. His right hand grips the round polished-brass doorknob and turns it; he is pulling the door open toward him, and it stands open about a hand's width, letting a warm shaft of morning sunlight in across his shoes and the floor. He glances out through the gap with a calm, contented half smile. He carries a folded tan raincoat over his left forearm and wears a brown felt fedora.

The man: early thirties, tall, broad-shouldered, square jaw, cleft chin, light-brown wavy hair neatly side-parted and combed back, clean-shaven, warm healthy complexion, an idealized all-American catalog model. Wallpaper with a small muted floral pattern in cream, sage and dusty rose; a mustard-yellow runner carpet on a honey-colored wooden floor; a small walnut hall table beside the door with an old red rotary telephone on it; a bentwood hat stand in the background.

Composition: a vertical plate. The man is seen full-length in three-quarter view; the door, his hand on the knob and the narrow gap of sunlight read clearly at the center of the picture. A clean, uncluttered catalog composition with calm open space.

Style: idealized and wholesome, flat areas of color with soft painterly shading and crisp edges, the slightly elongated, elegant proportions of 1940s fashion illustration. A warm, slightly faded period palette: mustard and golden yellows, denim and slate blues, tans, chocolate and saddle browns, brick red, cream and warm grays. Everything period-correct for the 1940s.
